# Numbered-video hook practice bank

Status: proposed editorial practice, not tested scripts or verified demand. Product-specific counts remain provisional. No first-person experience invented. These are candidate source/angle assignments; Inquiry requires an actual question receipt and High Demand requires research before either label is asserted as evidence.

## Candidate 1: 5 Ways to Find Contacts in HighLevel
Territory: CRM. Source candidate: Inquiry (CJ suggested topic, not evidence of audience demand). Angle: Process.
Opener: “Looking for one contact? Or everyone who asked about the same service? Those are different searches. Here are five ways to find contacts in HighLevel—and when to use each one.”
Screen text: ONE PERSON / A GROUP (slash is visual separator, not delivery notation).
Visual: one synthetic contact card expands to a group; cut to a verified lookup demonstration. Conceptual card animation labeled where necessary.
Promise: choose a lookup approach based on available information and desired result.
Gate: verify five distinct approaches. Avoid counting alternate buttons opening the same search as different methods.

## Candidate 2: 3 Ways to Create Contacts in HighLevel
Territory: CRM. Source candidate: Expertise pending CJ confirmation. Angle: Process.
Opener: “One new contact. A spreadsheet of contacts. Or someone filling out your form. Let's look at three ways to get those people into HighLevel.”
Screen text: ONE / MANY / INCOMING (visual labels, not pause cues).
Visual: three real demo inputs converge on a contact record. Start first method immediately after scope.
Promise: select an entry route for the situation.
Gate: verify manual creation, import and form behavior/current prerequisites; show resulting fields and duplicate caveats. Three is selected scope, not claim there are only three methods.

## Candidate 3: 5 Checks Before You Publish a HighLevel Workflow
Territory: Automation. Source candidate: Expertise pending CJ confirmation. Angle: Mistakes/process.
Opener: “The workflow is built. Before you publish it, let's check who can enter, what they'll receive, and what happens next. Here are five checks to run on your own setup.”
Screen text: READY TO PUBLISH?
Visual: real draft workflow with Publish control visible; highlight first check rather than fake failure alarm. No publish action needed for hook.
Promise: a bounded preflight checklist, not guarantee against every failure.
Candidate checks: entry criteria, recipients/permissions, message fields/links, timing/branches, controlled end-to-end test. Validate exact five checks against chosen scenario.
Gate: sender/recipient safety and reproducible sandbox flow; no client mutation.

## Candidate 4: 5 Questions Before You Import Contacts
Territory: Data readiness. Source candidate: Expertise. Angle: Questions.
Opener: “This spreadsheet has names and email addresses. But which records are already in your account—and what are you allowed to send them? Here are five questions to answer before importing.”
Screen text: BEFORE YOU IMPORT
Visual: synthetic spreadsheet; highlight existing-record match and a consent/source column. No invented platform outcome.
Promise: prepare data and intent before import.
Gate: distinguish consent to contact from ability to import; verify mapping/duplicate behavior, no legal guarantee.

## Candidate 5: 5 Details to Include in an AI Build Prompt
Territory: AI/business systems. Source candidate: Expertise. Angle: Process.
Opener: “Build me a workflow. That tells the AI what to make—but not what the workflow should actually do. Here are five details to give it before it starts.”
Screen text: WHAT SHOULD IT DO?
Visual: vague prompt gains five labeled inputs in spoken order; show genuine output comparison only if actually run.
Candidate details: triggering situation, intended person, desired result, steps/timing, limits/exceptions. Show one consistent scenario.
Promise: a better-specified brief, not guaranteed correct AI output.
Gate: clarify selected AI/product, verify supported behavior and test results before demonstrating claims.

## Candidate 6: 5 Things to Decide Before Building a Sales Pipeline
Territory: Sales systems. Source candidate: Expertise. Angle: Insights/questions.
Opener: “Before you name the columns, decide what has to happen for a lead to move forward. Here are five decisions to make before building your sales pipeline.”
Screen text: WHAT EARNS THE NEXT STAGE?
Visual: synthetic lead card moves only when a concrete qualifying event is shown; conceptual diagram, not fake automation execution.
Candidate decisions: stage entry evidence, next action, owner, follow-up timing, won/lost definition. Validate with a single business scenario.
Promise: purposeful stages, not a universal pipeline for every business.

## Editorial checks applied
- No unsupported scarcity, secrets, savings, dominance or personal stories.
- Each hook establishes an object/action rather than merely announcing five tips.
- Visual has one teaching job; no mandatory fast cuts or interrupt timer.
- Counts are scoped promises, not claims to enumerate every available platform feature.
- Do not choose all candidates: make a compact picker, verify selected content, then draft.
